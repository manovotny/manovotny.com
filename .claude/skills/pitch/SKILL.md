# Pitch

Evaluate a post idea against this site's editorial bar and deliver an honest verdict. Michael has explicitly asked for candor — "there'll be no hard feelings." A kind no now beats a stale post later. No flattery, no grade inflation.

## The bar

This site's best posts are experience-driven, not steps-driven: career decisions (breaking up with Flash), things built and shipped (WP Test), talks given (WordCamp Atlanta), honest starts (Press start). The 2026 revival discarded ten posts; the discards were how-to series, vendor reviews, and version-pinned tutorials.

## Evaluate every pitch on

1. **Only-you test.** Could a thousand other developers write this? Generic tutorials fail; posts carrying Michael's decisions, mistakes, or measurements pass.
2. **Shelf life.** Will a version bump or product change make it _actively wrong_? Version-pinned setup guides rot fastest (the PhpStorm/Xdebug tutorial died this way). Stories and decisions don't expire.
3. **Signal.** Does it evidence growth or engagement — a project, a talk, a position taken, a lesson learned?
4. **Buried story.** Reviews and tutorials often wrap a better post: the WP Engine review's real content was the Iowa Girl Eats scaling war story. If the pitch's value is the story inside it, say so.
5. **Traffic is not value.** Search clicks to stale content bounce. Never justify a post by its SEO potential alone.

## Verdict format

Lead with one of three verdicts, then reasoning against the criteria above, in prose:

- **Write it** — and name the angle that makes it only-his.
- **Reshape it** — the pitch wraps a better post; name the post it should become.
- **Skip it** — say why plainly. Offer no consolation rewrite unless one genuinely exists.

If information is missing to judge (no outcome yet, no personal stake identified), ask for it before delivering a verdict.
